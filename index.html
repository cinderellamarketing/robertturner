2 Email and Outlook▶

Each person connects their own email once, in two steps.

  • Connect your mailbox. In Capsule, go to My Preferences, then My Mailbox Connections, sign in with Outlook and consent on behalf of the organisation. Your email signature goes here too.
  • Add Capsule for Outlook. In Outlook, open an email, click the four squares at the top right, choose Add apps, search for Capsule, add Capsule for Outlook and connect your Capsule account.

With an email open, the Capsule sidebar shows whether the sender is already in Capsule. From there you can add them as a person and their organisation, then save the contact, or save it and store the conversation. For someone already in Capsule, you can store that email on their record.

  • Store only emails that matter to a project and that others may need. A thank-you reply or a BNI visitor email does not need storing.
  • An email sent from Capsule goes from your Outlook, lands in your Sent folder and reaches the recipient as an ordinary email.
  • Up to five email templates live under Account Settings, Email Templates. Fields such as First Name fill themselves in. A template belongs to people and organisations, opportunities or projects, and only appears on that kind of record. The one called template test is there to overwrite.
3 Contacts and tags▶

People and organisations are separate records, with each person linked to their organisation. A tag on a contact says what your relationship is.

  • The Specialism list is edited under Account Settings, Tags, where options can be added or removed.
  • Add a person with the Add button, or straight from an email in Outlook. Give them a tag and link them to their organisation.
  • On any record you can log an activity, such as a call, and attach files.
  • Comments on a note are for talking to each other. Type @ and a name to mention someone. They get an email and see a red dot in Capsule.
4 Tasks and who they are for▶

Tasks are your to-do list, set for a day rather than a time. Meetings at a set time stay in your Outlook calendar.

  • Four of you share two logins, so each task has a category for who it is for: Robert, Josie, Giulia or Mattia.
  • The calendar shows tasks by month, week or as a schedule. Filter it by category to see only your own tasks.
5 Enquiries: the sales pipeline▶

Each enquiry is an opportunity in the Enquiries pipeline, for both services. Create the contact first, then add the opportunity from their record, so it is linked with less typing. Its tag says which service it is: Architecture or Party wall.

MilestoneProbabilityStale after
New enquiry10%3 days
Call or site visit30%7 days
Fee proposal sent60%14 days
  • An opportunity with no activity for longer than its stale period shows a red line, so nothing goes quiet unnoticed.
  • Source records where the enquiry came from: BNI, Repeat, Web, Word of mouth or Other. Referred by records who sent it.
  • Expected value is optional. Fill details in as you learn them.
  • When an opportunity is lost, choose a lost reason: Price, Went with another firm, Project not going ahead or No response. It leaves the pipeline but stays in the list view.
  • Milestones, probabilities, stale periods and lost reasons can all be changed under Account Settings, Sales Pipeline.
  • The list view filters records, for example people tagged Client. Save a filter as a list and tick make list available to other users. Deleting a list only removes the view, never the records.
6 Projects and the board▶

When you mark an opportunity as won, Capsule offers to create a linked project, with the contacts and description carried across. Add it to the Projects board and give it a start date and an expected close date where you know them.

ServiceStages on the board
ArchitecturePlanning, Building Regulations, Health and Safety
Party wallParty wall: Notices, Party wall: Award
  • Each project is named with its reference number and the site address.
  • A project's tags say which service it is, Architecture or Party wall, and a job can carry both.
  • Each project records Source and Referred by, plus the dates Notices served and Award served for party wall jobs.
  • When a job is finished, or goes quiet, close the project rather than deleting it. It leaves the board but stays on the client's record, and it can be reopened if they come back.
  • Stages are edited under Account Settings, Project Boards.
7 Tracks▶

A track is a ready-made list of tasks applied to a project or an opportunity. You tick each task off as it is done, and most appear only once the one before is ticked off, so they come one at a time rather than all at once. More than one track can run on the same project.

  • Tracks are edited under Account Settings, Tracks, where tasks, timings and categories can be changed, added or reordered.
  • A change only applies to tracks started afterwards, not to tracks already running.
  • Working days keep tasks off weekends, so the architecture tracks use them. The party wall tracks count calendar days, because the statutory periods do.
8 Party wall notices▶

A party wall job can involve several adjoining owners, and each one is tracked separately. Tagging the project Adjoining owners adds these details for up to three owners:

  • Their name, and the date their notice was served
  • Their response: consent, dissent or no response
  • The date a further letter was posted
  • The date of a section 10(4)(b) appointment
  • Their surveyor

A fourth owner onwards goes in Further owners. The notice periods track reminds you to check responses 16 days after posting, and to make the section 10(4)(b) appointment 12 days after the further letter. The award expiry track reminds you at six months, and again at ten months, to tell the client that an award expires at twelve months if work has not started.

9 Invoicing▶

Invoicing reminders are tasks for Josie within the tracks. Architecture has a Stage 1 invoice at the end of planning and a Stage 2 invoice at the end of building regulations. Both party wall tracks have an Invoice task once the award is served. Josie ticks the task off once the invoice is out, so everyone can see it has been done. Extras quoted during a job are invoiced separately.

10 Reporting and goals▶
  • Starter includes basic reports on activity, users, and opportunities won and lost over time.
  • A goal tracks a target over a period and shows your progress on the home screen. On the call, Jaanika set one up as an example: five new opportunities a month in the Enquiries pipeline, which resets at the start of each month.
  • Goals can count back over past dates, and can be recalculated, duplicated or deleted.
11 Your existing data▶
  • Your projects and their contacts came in from the project spreadsheet Giulia sent, rather than being typed in one at a time. The clients carry the Client tag.
  • When a job goes quiet, close the project. If the client comes back, reopen it, with everything still there.
  • The GDPR spreadsheet is not needed. The information is on each record and searchable.
